The work

Two things, installed together

The agents are the visible part. The operating system is what makes them stick.

An agent is a piece of software that does a job you are currently doing by hand, or not doing at all. Missed-call text-back. Quotes chased on day three and day seven. Job sheets turned into invoices. Supplier prices checked across postcodes and sorted by price, age and aspect.

On its own that lasts about a month. So we also install the thing it plugs into: job profiles that say who owns what, a scorecard with numbers someone is accountable for, and quarterly priorities short enough to finish. It is EOS-inspired, and it is the unglamorous half of the work.

We still do SEO for local clients. We deliver it the same way — agentically, with the data scraped and compiled rather than guessed.

How it works

Three steps. You can stop after any of them.

01

Survey

A paid half-day mapping how work actually moves through your business: who does what, where it stalls, what the stall costs. You keep the map whether or not you go further. If the answer is that you don't need us, the map says so.

02

Install

One agreed price, upfront, stated before you commit. Our words are: for what you want, given these cost metrics, that would be X — does that number align with your expectations, or does it shock you? You answer that before anyone signs anything.

03

Maintain

A monthly fee for monitoring, fixes and tuning. Agents drift when your business changes, and your business changes. You get a named person to ring, not a ticket queue.

What we won't do

The short list

  • We won't quote before the survey. Anyone who prices your business without looking at it is guessing, and you will pay for the guess later.
  • We won't sell you an agent you don't need. Some of the work that comes out of a survey is a spreadsheet and a conversation. We will tell you when that is the answer.
  • We won't lock you in. The monthly fee cancels with a month's notice. You keep the job profiles, the scorecard and the map.
  • We won't put a number on this site we can't source. If a figure isn't confirmed by the client it named, it doesn't get printed.
